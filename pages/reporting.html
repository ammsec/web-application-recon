Reporting

A finding nobody can reproduce or understand is a finding that doesn't get fixed. This phase turns everything gathered so far into a report someone else can act on.

Why this phase matters

Whether it's a bug bounty submission or a client pentest report, the reader is usually not the person who ran the tools. A clear, reproducible write-up is what actually gets a bug triaged, fixed, and paid out — a vague one gets closed as "unable to reproduce."

Report Structure

The Essentials Structure

Every finding, regardless of format, needs these sections at minimum: 

1. Title            — short, specific (e.g. "Stored XSS in profile bio field")
2. Summary          — one paragraph: what it is and why it matters
3. Severity         — Critical / High / Medium / Low, with justification
4. Affected asset   — exact URL, endpoint, or parameter
5. Steps to reproduce — numbered, exact, no assumed context
6. Proof of concept — request/response, payload used, screenshot
7. Impact           — what an attacker can actually do with this
8. Remediation      — a concrete fix, not just "sanitize input"

Severity Quick Reference

Severity Typical Examples Rough Guidance
Critical RCE, full account takeover, database dump, auth bypass No user interaction needed, full compromise of data/system
High SQLi, stored XSS on sensitive pages, IDOR on private data Significant data exposure or partial compromise
Medium Reflected XSS, CSRF on non-critical actions, open redirect Limited impact or requires user interaction/social engineering
Low Missing security headers, verbose error messages, minor info leak Little to no direct impact on its own

For a formal, defensible score, run the finding through the CVSS 3.1 calculator rather than assigning severity by feel — most bug bounty programs expect a CVSS vector alongside the rating.

Markdown Report Template

report-template.md Template 
## Title
[Vulnerability type] in [affected component]

## Severity
[Critical / High / Medium / Low] — CVSS 3.1: [vector string]

## Summary
One paragraph describing the issue and why it matters.

## Affected Asset
- URL: https://target.com/vulnerable-endpoint
- Parameter: `id`

## Steps to Reproduce
1. Navigate to ...
2. Send the following request ...
3. Observe ...

## Proof of Concept
​```http
GET /endpoint?id=' OR '1'='1 HTTP/1.1
Host: target.com
​```

## Impact
What an attacker gains — data accessed, actions performed, scope of compromise.

## Remediation
Specific, actionable fix (e.g. "Use parameterized queries" not "sanitize input").

## References
- OWASP link, CWE ID, or relevant docs
